# 01 — Vision and creative direction

## Player fantasy

Be catastrophically underqualified but surprisingly resourceful. You are not secretly a trained surgeon or pilot; you are a friend group with too much confidence and one shared rent bill. The fun is moving from panic to a barely credible solution.

The world accepts absurd hiring decisions with complete seriousness. An agency sees “excellent with pressure” and sends you to a fire station. Clients are demanding, observant, and occasionally vain, but not omniscient.

The game should allow genuinely competent play. After enough practice, players learn the task systems. New lies and contract combinations then force them to improvise again. Do not preserve the premise by deliberately making controls unusable.

## Five design pillars

1. **Your lie becomes your problem.** Claims produce specific obligations and visible tradeoffs.
2. **A mistake is an opportunity for cooperation.** Someone repairs it, someone distracts the client, someone finds the missing part.
3. **The audience changes the task.** A quick hidden shortcut and a believable public demonstration are different solutions.
4. **Failure still creates a story.** Partial pay, public embarrassment, and debrief callbacks beat frequent instant restarts.
5. **Small places, many interactions.** One excellent banquet room beats a giant empty hotel.

## The original pitch: what stays, what changes

| Original idea | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Four unemployed friends | Keep; support 1–4 | Strong identity without blocking solo buyers |
| Random profession each day | Modify to three contract offers | Players need agency and a way to avoid exhausting repeats |
| Customers must never discover the fraud | Keep as a shift failure condition | Makes appearances matter |
| Completely random button pressing | Allow as a first reaction, reward learning | Pure randomness becomes frustrating after the joke wears off |
| Every profession imaginable | Backlog | Each profession needs assets, tasks, balance, audio, QA |
| Live AI job interviews | Experiment later | The mechanical promise works without paid inference or microphones |
| “Viral potential: 9/10” | Remove as evidence | A creative impression is not measurable sales validation |

## Distinctive systems to protect

### Résumé debt

Each accepted claim changes the next contract. “No manuals needed” rewards confidence but creates a suspicion event if the client sees you reading. Players select their own problem rather than receiving an arbitrary debuff.

Future extension: a successful claim can become an agency endorsement. It adds a tagged variant to future offers, never silently makes every future shift harder. Players can decline that contract. No persistent spiral where one early joke ruins a save.

### Cover-up work is real work

A knocked-over cake produces a mess and loses presentation quality. Moving the cake behind a screen can prevent discovery, but does not restore its score. Repairing it takes time. Distracting the planner creates that time, but consumes the distractor's attention.

This creates two truthful values: what the team actually accomplished and what the client has observed. Do not turn “pretending” into a button that erases mistakes.

### Face-saving challenges

At high suspicion the client asks for one concrete demonstration: “Show me the dessert table.” The team gets a countdown to put the visible scene in order. Successful recovery feels earned, unlike selecting a random persuasive dialogue option.

### The debrief tells the story back

Track a few explicit incidents and connect them to the chosen claim. Example: “Promised a spotless venue. Created six puddles. Cleaned five.” These lines are generated from counters and authored templates, not an LLM.

## Three possible directions considered

| Direction | Strength | Main problem | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Party minigame collection | Easy variety, instant comprehension | Jobs can feel unrelated and shallow | Reject as core |
| Detailed profession simulator | Deep mastery | Massive research and content scope; weak shared system | Reject |
| Social stealth + physical job tasks | A single hook across professions | Needs readable witnessing and recovery | Choose |

The social stealth is lightweight and comedic. This is not a stealth combat game, a dialogue RPG, or competitive hidden-role deduction. All human players are on the same side and know the premise.

## Why wedding crew first?

It exercises almost every core mechanic with inexpensive props: an observable room, a private service area, fragile arrangements, a guest-facing deadline, and a client who can be distracted. It can be funny without gore, fire simulation, vehicles, or deformable bodies.

The wedding may be less immediately spectacular than a burning aircraft. That is acceptable for internal validation. Before deciding the public demo job, compare the wedding slice with a very small fire-job graybox. Do not assume the least expensive prototype is automatically the strongest trailer.

## Session structure

Quick Shift offers a complete 10–12 minute session: short selection and briefing, eight-minute work period, short inspection and debrief. Career links five workdays into roughly 50–65 minutes and saves between shifts. These are target ranges, not measured duration.

The apartment is initially one screen with the job board, rent reminder, and readiness controls. A walkable apartment can be added after the core loop proves itself. Avoid making players walk around to access menus repeatedly.

## Creative example: a successful disaster

1. The group selects “We don't need instructions” for a pay bonus.
2. A player reads the manual safely in the service corridor and shouts a symbol sequence.
3. Another player plugs the decorative fountain into the audio station and trips the fictional stage breaker.
4. The planner approaches, attracted by the speaker pop.
5. Someone asks her to inspect the flowers, using a limited distraction opportunity.
6. The team resets the breaker and gets the correct tray into place.
7. The planner returns. The cake is upright, the microphone works, and one teammate is standing on a suspicious stain.
8. Final inspection uncovers the stain; the crew takes a deduction but finishes the job.

The story is an intended systemic possibility, not a cutscene script. Every action needs a real implementation and an observable consequence.

## What should not become the game

- Endless dialogue before anything happens.
- A grind for movement speed or essential tools.
- Mandatory microphone roleplay or fluent English.
- Four people waiting while one player completes a UI puzzle.
- Client NPCs seeing through walls or instantly knowing every broken prop.
- Random catastrophes with no warning or valid recovery.
- An AI conversation novelty with weak gameplay underneath.
- A full physical simulation of water, bodies, aircraft, or fire.
- Sabotaging teammates as the optimal way to earn rewards.

## Unproven creative assumptions

The lie system may feel like restrictive challenge modifiers rather than deception. Witness pressure may be more stressful than funny. Experienced groups may assign one person to permanent distraction duty. Three jobs may feel too small despite variation. All four are playtest questions, not problems more content automatically fixes.
